The CPI climbed steadily.

The Consumer Price Index tracks the price of a fixed basket of everyday goods — food, rent, transport, utilities. When the index rises, the same basket costs more dollars, so your money buys less. Same basket, more dollars The CPI rose from about 75 to 101 over the decade — roughly a third more to buy the identical goods. 🥖 +42% bread, 2015 → 2025 🥛 +34% full-cream milk 🍗 +28% chicken breast 🛒 ~+35% the average trolley overall

The real pain appears when wages lag prices.

Wage Price Index versus Inflation
In 2022–23 the orange inflation line jumps far above the green wage line — that gap is the cost-of-living shock.

When inflation climbs faster than wages, you go backwards in real terms — even a pay rise that lags prices is effectively a pay cut. WPI vs inflation WPI is how fast wages rise; inflation is how fast prices rise. Your daily coffee costs more than inflation suggests.

Annual CPI Growth vs Coffee Price Growth

The CPI is an average across hundreds of items, so any single one can hide inside it. Coffee is the perfect test: a small, frequent purchase almost everyone makes. Tracking it against the headline CPI shows how a familiar everyday price can run well ahead of the "official" rate of inflation. Why coffee vs CPI? CPI is the whole basket averaged; coffee is one lived item. When the orange bars top the yellow, your daily cup rose faster than inflation overall. Why it bites harder A 50¢ rise on a daily coffee is about $180 a year — small per cup, large per habit.

Beyond the weekly shop, the small luxuries crept up too. Streaming subscriptions and the humble Big Mac — a global yardstick for everyday prices — both climbed far faster than the broad rate of inflation over the decade.

The stock market vs your shopping basket.

ASX200 investors outpaced inflation significantly over the decade — but not everyone has access to investment markets. ASX200 An index of Australia's 200 largest listed companies — a common benchmark for how the share market is performing. Beating inflation When an asset grows faster than CPI, its real value rises. Gold wins. Cash quietly loses.

$100 Across the Decade: Gold vs Cash

Both started as $100 in 2015. A decade later they tell opposite stories — gold roughly doubled while idle cash quietly lost a quarter of what it could buy. More Australians are working multiple jobs.

When one wage stops covering the bills, people take a second. The share of Australians holding more than one job dipped in the 2020 lockdowns, then climbed to a record high — tracking the cost-of-living squeeze. What it measures The share of employed Australians working more than one job at once — a stress signal for household budgets. 5.1% COVID-19 low, mid-2020 6.7% highest on record, 2024
